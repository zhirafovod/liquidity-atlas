Key Models and Takes

Stock Market as Liquidity Sink / Financialization

Excess money flows into financial assets (stocks, bonds) rather than goods/services, inflating asset prices while CPI remains low. Observed in post-GFC QE eras. Supports Ilya's idea partially. Supported by empirical observations of QE leading to asset bubbles more than goods inflation initially.

Modern Monetary Theory (MMT)

Governments issuing their own fiat currency cannot go bankrupt in that currency; the real constraint is inflation/resources, not debt. Taxes destroy money to manage demand. Debt "doesn't matter" if productive capacity absorbs spending. Aligns with "debt is just a number." Proponents: Stephanie Kelton, Warren Mosler, L. Randall Wray, Bill Mitchell.

Quantity Theory of Money (QTM)

MV = PQ. Money growth in excess of output growth leads to inflation if velocity constant. Long-run holds, but short-run velocity and absorption into assets matter. Monetarists: Milton Friedman, Steve Hanke (money growth precedes asset then goods inflation with lags).

Cantillon Effects / Austrian School

New money enters unevenly; early recipients (banks, asset owners) benefit first, inflating assets before goods. Creates malinvestment and inequality. Challenges the idea that it can be painlessly absorbed forever. Modern: Peter Schiff, etc.

Modern Economists and Theories on Money Volume, Stock Markets, Economy

  • Stephanie Kelton (MMT): Debt/printing not problematic for sovereigns; inflation is the limit. Asset prices can rise without CPI if capacity allows. Taxes/spending manage excess.
  • Steve Hanke (Monetarist): Broad money growth drives inflation and asset prices with lags (assets first). Warns of bubbles from excess money.
  • Ray Dalio: Debt cycles; excessive debt leads to deleveraging. Money printing can delay but not prevent cycles.
  • Richard Werner: Credit creation theory; bank lending creates money, drives asset prices and growth differently than base money.
  • Lyn Alden: Distinguishes monetary inflation (M2), asset inflation, consumer inflation. Excess money often hits assets first.
  • John Cochrane, others: Critics of MMT; political incentives lead to overspending and inflation risks.
  • Austrian economists (Mises, Hayek influences): Artificial low rates/money creation distort capital allocation, create bubbles that must burst.

Contradictions and Challenges

  • Post-2020 US experience: Massive M2 surge (~40%) eventually caused high CPI inflation (peaked ~9%), not permanently absorbed by stocks/housing without spillover via wealth effects and demand.
  • Asset inflation is real for non-owners (housing unaffordability, retirement via stocks).
  • Interest on debt is a real fiscal burden (even if paid in fiat, requires more printing or taxes).
  • Velocity can change; if people spend more, inflation hits goods.
  • Productivity growth (AI) helps but has limits; resource and capacity constraints remain.
  • In Russia: Shallow financial markets mean money hits real assets/goods faster → higher CPI. Subsidized mortgages amplified real estate as sink.
  • Inequality: Benefits asset owners disproportionately (Cantillon).
  • Open economies: Capital flows, currency depreciation can transmit inflation.
  • Long-run: QTM tends to hold; excess money relative to real output devalues currency.

Ilya's idea captures an observed phenomenon (asset absorption of liquidity) but underestimates eventual spillovers, inequality, and the need for real productivity growth. Debt is "just a number" only until inflation or political constraints bind.
